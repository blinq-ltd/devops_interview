# Scenario 3 — Database connection incident and pooling

Following an increase in API replicas, users are intermittently receiving
connection timeouts. The database endpoint itself is reachable, and the
incident began during a deployment.

**Ideal end state**: the error rate returns to baseline under the same
load that was failing, with evidence showing exactly where the timeout
was occurring (application pool, network, pooler, or the database) — and
a canary of the application running through the supplied connection
pooler, recovering under that same load.

## (a) Existing resources

- **Database**: the same Postgres cluster from scenario 1
  (`interview-db-app` secret in `cnpg-database`). Your app from scenario 1
  (or the scenario 2 baseline) should already be deployed against it.
- **A connection pooler**: `interview-db-pooler`, a PgBouncer instance in
  the `cnpg-database` namespace, port 5432, transaction pooling mode.
- **Diagnostic access**: a superuser connection exists
  (`interview-db-superuser` secret, `cnpg-database` namespace) for
  inspecting database-side system views — ask if you need a connection
  run on your behalf.
- **`interview/prereq/3/scripts/synthetic-client.sh`**: a continuous HTTP
  client you can point at your deployed app, logging timestamp/status/
  latency to CSV — useful for establishing a baseline and verifying
  recovery.
- Your app's connection pool size is configurable via the `PG_POOL_MAX`
  environment variable (`apps/api/src/db.js`), default 10.
- Cluster-internal DNS names (`*.svc.cluster.local` or short service
  names) aren't resolvable directly from this workstation — you'll need
  `kubectl port-forward` or an in-cluster pod to reach database/pooler
  endpoints directly.

## (b) Scenario tasks

Investigate the incident and restore service. You decide what to look at,
what evidence distinguishes the possible causes, and what the right scope
of mitigation is. Once service is restored, move the application's traffic
through the supplied pooler and demonstrate it holds up under the same
load that was failing before.

## (c) Final outputs and proof of completion

- Evidence establishing where the timeout actually occurred, not just
  that it occurred.
- A before/after comparison (e.g. the synthetic client's log) showing the
  failure and the recovery.
- The application running through the pooler, with evidence it's actually
  using it (not falling back to a direct connection).
- Be ready to explain the scope of your mitigation and what you
  deliberately chose not to do.
